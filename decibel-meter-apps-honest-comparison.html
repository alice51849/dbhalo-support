Decibel meter apps for iPhone, compared honestly (dB Halo vs Decibel X, NIOSH SLM and others)

I make dB Halo. This page compares it with four popular sound meter apps on the US App Store, including where they are better than mine. Store data was read on November 26, 2026; ratings and prices change, so check each store page before you decide.

The short version

  • Want a free meter whose accuracy was tested in a NIOSH acoustics lab, with no purchases at all? NIOSH Sound Level Meter is hard to beat.
  • Want the most-reviewed app (about 164,000 US ratings), with iCloud history sync? Decibel X.
  • Want spectrum, noise dose, PDF/CSV export and calibration profiles with a one-time purchase instead of a subscription? That is the gap dB Halo tries to fill.
  • Want a simple reading you can share in a message? Decibel Meter Sound Detector.

Where the others are better than dB Halo

  • Track record. dB Halo had 1 US rating when I checked. Decibel X, NIOSH SLM and the others each have more than 17,000. If you want an app many people have already tested, pick one of them.
  • Validated accuracy. NIOSH SLM’s description says it was “tested and validated (accuracy ± 2 dBA) according to standards in a reverberant chamber at NIOSH acoustics lab”. dB Halo makes no such claim. Its own description says measurements are estimates and it is not a certified sound level meter.
  • Completely free. NIOSH SLM lists no in-app purchases at all. In dB Halo, exports, calibration profiles, spectrum, noise dose and the Sleep Journal need the one-time Lifetime Pro purchase after a few free uses.
  • Deeper analysis options. Decibel : dB sound level meter offers FFT sizes up to 16,384 points and window functions. Decibel X lists ITU-R 468 weighting and SLOW / FAST / IMPULSE response times. dB Halo does not list these.
  • Sync. Decibel X syncs history through iCloud. dB Halo’s description does not mention sync.
  • More weighting options. Decibel X lists ITU-R 468, A, B, C and Z, and Decibel Meter Sound Detector lists A, B, C and Z. dB Halo lists A, C and Z.

Where dB Halo may fit better

  • One purchase, no subscription. “Lifetime Pro is one non-consumable in-app purchase: pay once, own it forever, with no subscription.” Basic sound level measurement, widgets, external microphones, Apple Watch and saving to Apple Health are free with no limits (quote: “Sound level measurement is always free with no limits, including widgets, external microphones, and Apple Watch, and so is saving levels to Apple Health”).
  • Several tools in one session. “FFT Spectrum, RTA octave bands and Spectrogram share one live session.”
  • Logs for a noise problem. “a time-stamped log of loud events”, plus “export a PDF report or CSV” and “burn dB overlays onto photos and videos”.
  • Night sound log without saving raw audio. The Sleep Journal “recognizes and classifies snoring, breathing, speech, coughing, alarms, animal sounds, impacts, ambient sound and crying, then logs each event without saving raw microphone audio.” It is a sound log, not a medical tool.
  • Privacy. “There is no account, no ads, no analytics, and no tracking. Processing stays on your devices.”

Who should not use dB Halo

  • Anyone who needs a certified sound level meter or a workplace noise assessment. Use a calibrated Class 1 or Class 2 meter. dB Halo’s description says it “does not replace medical advice or an occupational safety determination.”
  • Anyone who wants every tool free forever. NIOSH SLM is the better fit.
  • Anyone who needs history synced across devices right now.

How this comparison was made

  • “Not in description” means the store description does not mention it. The app may still have it.
