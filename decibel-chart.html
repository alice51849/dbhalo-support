Decibel chart of common sounds

A whisper is about 30 dB, normal conversation 60–70 dB, a vacuum cleaner 70–80 dB, a hair dryer 80–90 dB and a concert roughly 94–110 dB. From around 85 dB, how long you listen starts to matter for your hearing. The chart below gives approximate ranges, explains what the numbers mean, and shows how to take a reading with an iPhone that you can actually compare.

How to read decibels: why 10 dB is a big step

Decibels are logarithmic. That is why the numbers feel strange at first:

  • +3 dB means roughly twice the sound energy. Two identical vacuum cleaners running side by side read about 3 dB higher than one.
  • +10 dB is usually perceived as about twice as loud. A 70 dB room sounds roughly twice as loud as a 60 dB room.
  • Distance matters. For a small source in open space, doubling the distance lowers the level by about 6 dB. Indoors the drop is smaller because walls reflect sound.

Most charts, including this one, use A-weighting, written dB(A) or dBA. It reduces very low and very high frequencies to match how human ears hear at normal levels. C-weighting keeps much more of the bass, so it is useful for thumping music, engines or the low hum of ventilation. Z-weighting is flat with no correction. If a bass-heavy noise bothers you but the dB(A) reading looks low, compare it with dB(C).

Hearing damage depends on both level and time. Two widely used occupational references from the United States:

NIOSH uses a 3 dB exchange rate (every +3 dB halves the time), OSHA uses 5 dB. Both are workplace criteria for adults, not targets for a bedroom or a child's toy. For everyday life the practical rule is simple: if you have to shout to be understood at arm's length, the level is probably above 85 dB, and shorter is better. If you notice ringing ears or muffled hearing after an event, talk to a doctor or audiologist.

How to measure noise correctly with a phone

A phone reading is only useful if you can repeat it. Use the same method every time:

  1. Distance: decide on a fixed distance, for example 1 m from a machine or the middle of the room where you hear the noise. Write it down.
  2. Direction: point the bottom microphone of the iPhone toward the source and keep your hand, case and body out of the way. Do not cover the microphone.
  3. Duration: for steady noise (fans, traffic) measure at least 1–5 minutes. For changing noise (music, footsteps, construction) measure the whole episode and note the average (LAeq) and the maximum.
  4. Background: also measure the same spot when the noise is off. A reading of 45 dB means more if the room is normally 30 dB.
  5. Time and context: record date, start and end time, which room, and whether windows were open.
  6. Avoid artefacts: no wind on the microphone, no talking, no tapping the phone, and keep the phone still on a soft surface or in your hand.

How accurate is a phone sound meter?

Phone microphones are designed for speech, not for laboratory measurement. Expect a few dB of difference compared with a certified meter, more at very low frequencies and at very high levels where the microphone or its processing may limit the signal. Accuracy improves when you:

  • compare your phone against a reference meter once and save the offset as a calibration profile;
  • use a measurement-grade external microphone;
  • compare readings made with the same device and the same method, rather than across different phones.

For trends, comparisons and personal awareness a phone works well. For anything with legal or medical consequences, use a phone log as supporting evidence and rely on official or certified measurements.

Frequently asked questions

How many decibels is too loud?

Many occupational references treat 85 dBA averaged over 8 hours as the point where hearing protection becomes important. Above that, safe time drops quickly: with the NIOSH 3 dB rule, 94 dBA is about 1 hour and 100 dBA about 15 minutes.

Is 60 dB loud?

No. 60 dB is roughly normal conversation at 1 m. It can still be annoying at night in a quiet bedroom, because what disturbs sleep depends on the background level and the type of sound.

Why does my phone show a different number than my friend's phone?

Different microphones, cases, weightings (A vs C), and distances all shift the reading. Compare like with like: same phone, same spot, same weighting, same duration.

What is LAeq?

LAeq is the A-weighted equivalent continuous level: one number that represents the energy average of a changing noise over the measurement period. It is the figure most noise guidance refers to.
