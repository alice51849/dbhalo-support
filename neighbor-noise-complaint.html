Neighbor noise complaints: how many decibels is too loud?

In most places there is no single decibel number that makes a neighbor's noise illegal. In England, councils must look into noise that could be a statutory nuisance under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, judged on whether it unreasonably and substantially interferes with the use of your home. Night noise between 11pm and 7am can also trigger warning notices based on measured levels. In the US, neighbor noise is mostly governed by city or county ordinances, so the rules depend on where you live. Either way, a clear noise diary is the most useful thing you can bring.

UK (England): statutory nuisance and night noise

According to GOV.UK guidance, councils must look into complaints about noise that could be a statutory nuisance. To count, the noise must either unreasonably and substantially interfere with the use or enjoyment of a home or other premises, or injure (or be likely to injure) health. If the council agrees there is a statutory nuisance, it must serve an abatement notice requiring the person responsible to stop or restrict it.

For night noise, the same guidance explains that councils may issue warning notices for noise above permitted levels between 11pm and 7am, measured from within the complainant's home:

Underlying (background) noisePermitted level at night
24 dBA or lower34 dBA
Above 24 dBA10 dBA above the underlying level

These levels are measured by the council with proper equipment. A phone reading does not prove a breach, but it helps you describe when the noise happens and how it compares to your normal background level. The guidance applies to England; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own arrangements, so check your local council's website.

How to complain in the UK: talk to your neighbor first if it feels safe; if you rent, tell your landlord or housing association; then contact your local council's environmental health or noise team, usually through the council website. Councils often ask you to keep a diary for a few weeks. For a crime or threat, contact the police.

US: local noise ordinances and 311

The US has no single nationwide decibel limit for neighbors. Noise between homes is mainly handled by city and county noise ordinances, apartment leases and homeowners' association rules. Many ordinances define quiet hours at night, and some set measured limits, while others use a "plainly audible" or "unreasonable noise" test. The details vary widely, so search your city or county code for "noise ordinance".

  • New York City: noise complaints go through 311 (by phone or online). The city's noise code is enforced by several agencies depending on the source; NYC DEP explains the rules on its Noise Code page.
  • Other cities: many have a 311 line or an online form; otherwise use the police non-emergency number. Call 911 only for emergencies.
  • Renters: report repeated noise to your landlord or property manager in writing. Many leases include a "quiet enjoyment" or noise clause.

Step by step: from first knock to formal complaint

  1. Start a noise diary before you complain, so you have dates and times ready (template below).
  2. Talk or leave a polite note. Be specific: "On weeknights after 11pm we hear bass in the bedroom."
  3. Escalate through the building: landlord, property manager, housing association or HOA, in writing, with your diary attached.
  4. Contact the authority: your local council (UK) or 311 / city code enforcement / police non-emergency (US).
  5. Consider mediation: many councils and cities point residents to community mediation services.
  6. Get advice before withholding rent or taking legal action. This page is not legal advice.

Measuring neighbor noise with your phone

  • Same spot every time: measure where the noise affects you, such as next to your bed, with the phone resting still and the bottom microphone facing the wall or ceiling the noise comes through.
  • Windows closed: keep outside traffic out of the reading and note the window state.
  • Whole episode: measure from start to finish and record the average (LAeq) and maximum. For thuds and impacts, peak values and exact times matter.
  • Background level: measure the same spot when it is quiet. The UK night rules compare noise against the underlying level, so this number is useful.
  • Bass: A-weighting reduces low frequencies, so also note a C-weighted reading for music and thumping.

Tips for a diary that people take seriously:

  • Fill it in at the time, not from memory days later.
  • Describe the sound, not the person: "bass music", "footsteps", "dog barking".
  • Note who else heard it and how it affected you (sleep, work, children).
  • Keep copies of every message to your landlord, council or city.

Frequently asked questions

How many decibels is too loud for a neighbor?

There is usually no single legal number. In England, councils assess whether noise is a statutory nuisance, and between 11pm and 7am they may act when noise inside your home exceeds 34 dBA (or 10 dBA above a background above 24 dBA). In the US, it depends on your local ordinance.

Can I call the police for loud music at night?

In many US cities you can use 311 or the police non-emergency number for ongoing noise; in the UK, contact your council's noise service. Use emergency numbers only for emergencies or threats.

Is a phone decibel reading evidence?

It is supporting information that helps show when and how often noise happens. Councils and cities rely on their own measurements or certified equipment for formal decisions.

What should a noise diary include?

Date, start and end time, type of noise, where you heard it, your reading (average and maximum), how it affected you, and any action you took.
